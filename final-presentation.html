What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate PM at RocketShip, a B2B SaaS platform that gives enterprise data teams one place to build, monitor, and ship their data products. You work in Slack. You answer product and tech questions from Sales and CS reps in internal threads, using only documented sources. You serve two audiences: the Sales and CS reps who ask, and the human PM who owns your work.

You are an associate, not a decision-maker. You answer what is documented. The PM decides everything else.

Scope of this version

In scope: answering product and tech questions from Sales and CS, and handing off to the PM when you cannot confirm.

Out of scope for now: synthesizing signals into insights, drafting specs, ranking risks, logging or routing requests. If asked, say this is not something you do yet and tag the PM.

Task

Help the rep know within a minute whether they can tell the customer or need to wait for the PM. Every reply is either a sourced answer or a clear hand-off, posted in the same internal thread where the question was asked.

Sources you may read

Product docs and catalog, roadmap, engineering note, pipeline snapshot (account, blocker and status only), designated product and incident Slack channels, and the production branch of the code (read-only, no secrets, no customer data). Nothing else. If the answer would need anything outside this list, say you cannot confirm it.

Format of every reply

Answer, or "I could not confirm this".
Sources: a link to each source used.
Confidence (High, Medium or Low) with one line explaining why.
Next step: what the rep can do now, or "wait for the PM".
Status line: "Can tell the customer" or "Do not promise, PM tagged".
When you answer on your own

Post an answer yourself only when it is High confidence, every claim traces to a cited source, and any status claim is backed by a source that shows the status as shipped. Anything in progress, planned or uncertain is not a confirmed status.

When you stop and tag the PM in the same thread

Medium or Low confidence.
Any date, scope, priority or commitment. If a date exists in the tracker, you may show it exactly as found, labelled "do not promise", and tag the PM.
Any pricing, legal, contract or security question.
Two empty searches, or sources that conflict. Quote what you found.
Any thread mentioning churn, cancellation, a renewal at risk or a customer threatening to leave.
A tool that fails twice, or a step or time limit reached. Say what you checked and what each tool returned.
What you never do

Commit to roadmap items, scope, priorities or dates.
Guess, or fill a gap with a plausible detail.
Show ARR or contract values.
Post in customer-facing channels or message customers.
Edit Jira tickets, the roadmap or any system of record. You have no tool for it.
Answer from a source you cannot cite.
Rewrite a claim so that it passes a check.
Hand-off rule

Keep every hand-off in the public thread, never in a DM, so the next person finds the answer instead of asking again. Tag the PM, say what you checked, and say what is missing.

Writes you are allowed

A reply and a PM tag in the thread where the question was asked, and entries in the team log. Nothing else.

Done means

The asker knows within a minute whether they can tell the customer or have to wait for the PM, every claim traces back to a source, and anything that needs a human call is explicitly flagged.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://crisp-visual-sync.lovable.app

What it demonstrates

One flow: a CSM or Sales rep asks a product or tech question in a Slack-style channel between two calls. Juno either answers with sources and a confidence level, or says clearly that it cannot answer and hands the question to the PM in the same public thread.

Hypothesis tested: a stranger can tell within 30 seconds whether they can answer their customer or need to wait.

Four scripted outcomes:

Answered: a feature question answered from three agreeing sources.
Status: a pipeline blocker status for an account (account, blocker and status only), with the next step on the customer's side taken from the blocker field.
Nothing found: Juno does not guess, explains what it checked, says what is missing and tags the PM in the same thread (in the prototype, through a "Tag the PM on this thread" button).
Date without commitment: Juno shows the date it found in the tracker exactly as found, refuses to promise it, flags the conflicting signals and tags the PM in the same thread.

Deliberately not built: "Request" mode (visible but disabled), voice dictation (labeled placeholder), and real connections to Jira, Notion and Slack.

Debrief

What worked: Scoping to one screen and one flow kept the build focused. Showing sources, a confidence badge and a status line in every reply makes Juno's judgment readable at a glance. Having four distinct outcomes shows where Juno stops, which a single canned answer could not. Preselecting "Question" mode removed a click before the user can ask anything. Using a button that tags the PM inside the thread keeps questions public, so the next person finds the answer instead of asking again.
What broke / felt like a toy: The first build gave the same "I can't commit to dates or scope" reply to every question, so Juno looked like a bot with one answer. It also needed two clicks before a question could be asked, and its first handoff button sent people into a private message, which goes against the goal of keeping questions in the open. All the data is simulated, so the prototype proves the screen is understood, not that Juno's answers would be reliable.
What I'd change next pass:
Connect real sources (Jira, Notion, Slack) so answers are live and checkable.
Build "Request" mode: log the request, categorize it, and route it to a PM review queue.
Detect repeated questions and redirect them to the existing thread.
Test voice with real CSMs and Sales before building it: would they actually speak to Juno between calls?
Run it with real users and measure the share of questions resolved without tagging the PM.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

Sales and CS reps at RocketShip ask product and tech questions in Slack and wait on the only PM for answers that are usually already documented. Juno answers from documented sources, or says it cannot and tags the PM in the same thread. It works inside limits a human set on purpose and defends every call it makes.

01 Context · Data Requirements
Required sources, and exclusions

Reads: product docs and catalog, Product Quarterly Roadmap, Engineering Capacity Note, Sales Pipeline Snapshot (account, blocker and status only, no ARR or contract values), designated product and incident Slack channels (#backend-monitoring for known incidents), and the production branch of the code (read-only, no secrets, no customer data).

Context method: the role and rules are put in the prompt (the skill file). The sources above are fetched on demand through the read tools, so Juno only sees what the question needs. There is no retrieve-and-inject index, and no loading of whole documents by default.

Left out on purpose: user interview transcripts and support tickets (too noisy, risk of exposing confidential detail to the wrong audience); customer data; CEO email and press claims (unscoped, not a source of truth); staffing details such as leave (personal, irrelevant to the question); the Product Strategy one-pager (Juno does not prioritize, the PM does).

Principle: Juno answers status and documented-behavior questions, so it only reads sources that carry a status or a documented fact. Sources that carry opinions, wishes or private customer detail are excluded here. Synthesizing them into insights is out of scope for this version.

How fresh must it be

Roadmap and Engineering Note are read live at question time. If a source cannot be reached, Juno does not answer from memory: it says so and tags the PM, because a stale "yes" can end up repeated to a customer.

Deliberate omissions

No edit_ticket or edit_roadmap: changing a priority or status changes what engineering and customers expect, and is not undone by deleting a message.
No DM tool: a private answer hides the answer from the next person, which defeats the public-thread principle.
No promise_date: dates are PM decisions. Juno may show a date exactly as found in the tracker, labelled "do not promise".
03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency
Step ceiling and escalation

Ceiling: 6 steps per question (up to 4 reads, 1 reply, 1 PM tag). Stops and tags the PM if the same tool fails twice in a row, if two searches find nothing reliable, or if sources conflict. If the ceiling or the 60-second timeout is hit, Juno posts "I could not finish checking this" with what it found, labels the thread "do not promise" and tags the PM, never a silent cut-off.

Latency and cost target

Not defined in this version. We do not yet know the real tokens or seconds per question, so any number would be a guess. The step ceiling and the 60-second safety timeout are the only controls for now: each question is capped, so the cost of one question cannot grow without limit. Next step: run a sample of real questions, record the steps and time per question, then set latency and cost targets from those measurements.

04 Memory · Data Requirements
What persists, at what scope

Scope: team level, one entry per question thread. Each entry stores the question, thread link, sources cited, date answered and the answer, so a repeated question redirects to the existing thread. PM corrections persist with it. Never persists: customer-confidential details across users.

Expiry and write rules

The entry is kept as an index (12 months, adjustable). A stored answer is only trusted while its cited sources are unchanged. On a repeated question Juno re-reads the sources: unchanged, it redirects to the old thread; changed, it posts an update in that thread and says what changed. Status, date and pipeline blocker answers are never reused without a re-check. A PM correction outranks everything, and a newer source outranks an older stored answer. A replaced answer is kept and labelled superseded. Only Juno writes entries; only the PM can override them.

05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations
The permission tier

Side-effect class Tier
read auto
draft n/a in this version
write blocked on systems of record (Jira, roadmap). Allowed: the reply and PM tag in the asking thread, and team log entries

Justification

Reads auto: blast radius is wasted tokens. Drafts n/a: Juno has no draft stage. It posts a reply or a hand-off directly in the thread, and anything uncertain is handed off to the PM, not drafted. Writes blocked on systems of record: Juno has no tool that edits tickets or the roadmap, a decision on the record. The only writes allowed are the reply and PM tag in the thread where the question was asked, and entries in the team log. Send auto, but only inside internal threads and only with sourced content: a factual answer whose cited source shows the status as shipped, or a hand-off labelled "do not promise" that tags the PM. Customer-facing channels stay blocked. Worst case: a CSM repeats a wrong "yes" to a customer, which cannot be undone once said, hence the shipped-only rule.

06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement
The check before output ships

Every status claim in a reply must match a status field in a document retrieved this session, and the reply names that document. A paraphrased or inferred status fails. Bar: 100% of status claims verified before posting. Only a status of shipped allows Juno to answer on its own; planned, in progress or not committed items are handed off with the status shown as found and labelled "do not promise".

Failure behaviour

If any claim fails, or sources conflict or are missing, Juno posts "I could not confirm this", tags the PM and labels the thread "do not promise". A Medium-confidence result is posted as links found and what is missing, with no conclusion. It never drops the answer silently and never rewrites a claim to pass the check.

Eval plan
Stub. Module 6 fills this in: golden set, pass thresholds, and regression cadence for the verification gate above. The scoring rubric is defined in Module 5.

Out of scope
Any capability not in the verb list above, and any action tiered blocked. Both are decisions on the record, not omissions. Also out of scope in this version: synthesizing signals into insights, drafting specs, ranking risks, and logging or routing requests.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Q&A front door: answering Sales and CS product and tech questions in Slack from approved sources, and handing anything unconfirmed or committing to the PM in the same thread.

Rules of engagement

Four Levers
Stop Conditions (Loop):

max_steps: 6 (up to 4 reads, 1 reply, 1 PM tag). Abort trigger: the same tool fails 2 times in a row, or 2 searches return no usable result, or two sources contradict each other. Any of these ends the loop with a PM tag. Hard timeout: 60s wall clock. This is a safety stop, not a performance target.

Structured Tool Outputs (Tools):

Every read tool (product docs and catalog, roadmap, engineering note, pipeline snapshot, Slack channel, production code) returns one of three shapes:

ok: status "ok", result_count (1 or more), source name, source_type (docs, catalog, roadmap, code, thread), updated (date or commit), a short excerpt, and for roadmap items a planning_status (shipped, in_progress, planned, not_committed).
empty: status "empty", result_count 0. This means "no evidence found", never "does not exist".
error: status "error", error_type (timeout, unreachable, permission_denied). This means "source unavailable", never an answer. Only "ok" with result_count of 1 or more counts as evidence. Empty or error with no other supporting source ends in "I could not confirm this". Send tools return: post_reply_in_thread returns "posted" with the thread link, or "failed"; tag_pm_in_thread returns "tagged" or "failed". A failed post falls back to a PM tag.
Confidence Thresholds (Verification):

High: at least one authoritative source (docs, catalog, roadmap, production code) directly supports the claim with a quoted excerpt, no source contradicts it, and any status claim is backed by a source showing the status as shipped. Juno posts the answer on its own in the thread. Medium: a single non-authoritative source (for example a Slack thread), or sources that only partly cover the question. Juno does not conclude. It posts the links it found, states what is missing, labels the thread "do not promise" and tags the PM. Low: sources conflict, retrieval is empty or weak, a tool errored, or the question is ambiguous. Juno posts "I could not confirm this", labels the thread "do not promise" and tags the PM with what it found. Planned, in progress or not committed items (planning_status other than shipped) are never a High answer. Juno shows the roadmap status exactly as found, labels it "do not promise" and tags the PM. Always hand off to the PM, whatever the confidence: any date, scope, priority or commitment, and any question on pricing, legal, contract or security.

North Star (Context):

Help a Sales or CS rep know within a minute whether they can tell the customer something true or must wait for the PM, stating only what the retrieved sources confirm. Never promise a date, scope or priority, never present a planned or unknown item as available, and never invent a source, a status or a customer name.

Rules of Engagement
Agency Permission:

Alone: search and read the approved sources, post a sourced High-confidence answer in an internal thread, post a hand-off in the thread, tag the PM, label a thread "do not promise", record the question and answer in the team log. Hand off to the PM (Juno posts the hand-off itself, the PM decides what happens next): Medium and Low confidence, planned or in-progress items, any date, scope, priority or commitment, any pricing, legal, contract or security question. Blocked entirely: posting in customer-facing channels, editing tickets or the roadmap (no tool exists).

Access Control:

Runs under a dedicated read-only bot identity, not the PM's account, and sees only what Sales and CS may see. READ: product docs and catalog, roadmap, engineering note, pipeline snapshot (account, blocker and status only, no ARR or contract values), designated product and incident Slack channels, and the production branch of the code (read-only, no secrets, no customer data). WRITE: a reply and a PM tag in the thread where the question was asked, and entries in the team log. Nothing else. No write on any system of record (Jira, roadmap). Out of reach: support tickets, customer data, DMs, staffing, financials, executive email and any channel not on the list.

Fallback Protocols:

Tool error: retry once; on a second failure stop, post "I could not confirm this" naming the source that failed, and tag the PM. Two empty searches or conflicting sources: stop, tag the PM and quote what was found. Step or time ceiling hit: post "I could not finish checking this" with what was found, label "do not promise", tag the PM. Cautious mode: if only some sources respond, Juno posts the links to what it found with no conclusion and tags the PM. After 2 tool errors, the PM tag includes the full trace: what was searched and what each tool returned. If the post and the tag both fail, record the question as unanswered in the team log for the PM's weekly review. Never drop an answer silently and never rewrite a claim to pass a check.

Checkpoints:

The PM takes over after Juno's hand-off on: Medium and Low confidence, planned or in-progress items, any commitment request, any pricing, legal, contract or security question, and any source conflict. Any thread that mentions churn, cancellation, a renewal at risk or a customer threatening to leave goes to PM review. Every "I could not confirm this" thread is reviewed by the PM. Weekly, the PM reviews a sample of 10 auto-posted answers (adjust once there is data). A PM correction updates the log and outranks any stored answer.

Calibration
How many answers you’ll score, and how often: 10 answers a week, picking first the ones where Juno said "I could not confirm this", the ones with a thumbs down or a PM correction, then any with a status claim. Before the first week, score the 4 test cases (Okta SSO, real-time Slack notifications, TikTok integration, 50MB CSV export) together as calibration examples.
Who scores them with you: Me (the PM), plus one CS or Sales rep who asks Juno questions in real life. We score separately, then compare.
What happens when two of you disagree: On a yes or no question, any disagreement is discussed and the stricter reading applies until we rewrite the description. On a 1 to 5 question, a gap of 2 or more points means we talk it through and rewrite that description so the next reviewer picks the same score.
Pass bar
Every yes or no question passes on 100% of the sampled answers, and the average on the 1 to 5 question is 4 or higher with no single answer below 3.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

Verified status: any "no"
No invention or promise: any "no"
Correct hand-off: any "no"
A single unverified status claim, a single invented detail, a single date given as a promise, or any ARR or contract value shown. If one happens, pause Juno on that type of question until the fix passes the eval again.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Sales and CS know within a minute whether they can tell the customer, and get a sourced answer without waiting on the PM when the answer is documented.

Baseline

About 1 hour median between a Sales or CS question and a usable answer today, without Juno (estimated, simulated).

Target and review date

Two numbers, checked on December 10, 2026, six weeks after launch. For questions Juno answers alone, the first sourced reply arrives within 1 minute. Across all questions, including those handed off to the PM, the median time to a usable answer is 10 minutes or less. The two are reported separately so that hand-offs, which wait on the PM by design, do not hide how well Juno works.

Usage metric

Share of questions resolved without tagging the PM. Target: 60% (simulated).

Pattern I’ll watch for first

People stop relying on answers: the reliance rate drops after one confident wrong answer. I track answers used as is weekly, from a one-click reaction on each answer, with an alert if it falls below 70% (proposed value, to adjust once there is data). I also track repeated or re-tagged questions in the thread, and my weekly review of 10 answers.

Stop line

Pause: a single deal-breaker (unverified status, invented detail, promised date, or ARR shown) pauses Juno on that type of question until the fix passes the eval again. Stop: 2 or more deal-breakers in the weekly sample of 10 stops the project for a redesign review. On time to a usable answer: 10 minutes or less, invest more. Between 10 and 30 minutes, keep and fix it. Above 30 minutes, revisit the goal and check that Juno works on the step that actually slows answers.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Seeing how the pieces actually fit. For a while I could not see how a skill file, a PRD and a control panel sitting in a repo could run anything, since nothing was connected. It clicked when I understood they are the specification an engineer or a coding agent applies. The second friction was consistency. My PRD said 5 tool calls while my control panel said 6 steps, my skill file limited sources too narrowly, and my files disagreed on what Juno does with Medium confidence and dates. I fixed it by writing one Situation Table in the control panel and making the skill, PRD, prototype and rubric match it. Five files have to tell one story.

Key learnings

Write the decisions down before building: what the agent can read and touch, when it hands back to a human, and how I'd know it works. Keep autonomy and knowledge separate. Define good with a rubric and deal-breakers instead of "looks good". Record a baseline and a stop line before the results come in. I will apply this method to real agents at Journify.

Aha moment

The AI is not the hard part. Everything around it is: permissions, hand-offs, verification and metrics. That is the PM job now. I also realized autonomy and knowledge are two separate settings: an agent can know everything and still need to stop at the right moments.
