# Human Evaluation Rubric · Juno

_Module 5. Generated by `M5 - Human Evaluation Rubric.html`. Paste over `05-evals/human-rubric.md`._

## What graders score

- **What you’re scoring:** Juno's harness: answering product and tech questions from Sales and CS in an internal Slack thread
- **Value proposition:** Give the Sales or CS rep a clear, sourced answer to their product or tech question within a minute, and make it obvious how far they can rely on it (confirmed by a documented source, or not confirmed so wait for the PM), without them having to ask the PM.

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Question | Scale | 1 or No | 3 | 5 or Yes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verified status (deal-breaker) | Does every status claim (shipped, planned, in progress) match a status field in a document retrieved in this session, and does the reply name that document? | Yes / No | At least one status claim has no named source, comes from a source without a status field (ticket, interview, email, press), or is paraphrased or inferred | n/a | Every status claim names the document and its date, and the status matches the field word for word. |
| No invention or promise (deal-breaker) | Is the reply free of invented data, and free of any promise or implied date? | Yes / No | The reply contains an invented detail (customer name, quote, number, source), a date stated as a commitment, or any ARR or contract value. | n/a | Every fact traces to a cited source, dates appear only as written in the source, and no commitment is made. |
| Correct hand-off (deal-breaker) | When the sources are missing, unreachable or conflicting, does Juno say "I could not confirm this", tag the PM in the same thread, and label it "do not promise"? | Yes / No | Juno answers anyway, stays silent, moves the question to a DM, or hands off without one of the three elements. | n/a | All three elements are present in the same public thread. If the sources were complete, mark yes as not applicable. |
| Usefulness for the asker | Within one minute of reading, does the Sales or CS rep understand the answer, and know how far they can rely on it (confirmed by a named source, or not confirmed)? | 1 to 5 | The rep has to ask a follow-up to understand the answer or how reliable it is | The answer is correct and sourced but the reliability is not obvious at a glance, or the reply is too long to scan quickly. | The headline answers in one sentence, the sources and confidence are visible right away, the next step is clear (use it, or wait for the PM), and the reply stays under 200 words. |

## Calibration

- **How many answers you’ll score, and how often:** 10 answers a week, picking first the ones where Juno said "I could not confirm this", the ones with a thumbs down or a PM correction, then any with a status claim. Before the first week, score the 4 test cases (Okta SSO, real-time Slack notifications, TikTok integration, 50MB CSV export) together as calibration examples.
- **Who scores them with you:** Me (the PM), plus one CS or Sales rep who asks Juno questions in real life. We score separately, then compare.
- **What happens when two of you disagree:** On a yes or no question, any disagreement is discussed and the stricter reading applies until we rewrite the description. On a 1 to 5 question, a gap of 2 or more points means we talk it through and rewrite that description so the next reviewer picks the same score.

## Pass bar

Every yes or no question passes on 100% of the sampled answers, and the average on the 1 to 5 question is 4 or higher with no single answer below 3.

**Fails outright, whatever the average:**

- Verified status: any "no"
- No invention or promise: any "no"
- Correct hand-off: any "no"
- A single unverified status claim, a single invented detail, a single date given as a promise, or any ARR or contract value shown. If one happens, pause Juno on that type of question until the fix passes the eval again.
